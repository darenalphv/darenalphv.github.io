# 05 · Benchmark: what makes top-tier consulting decks work

This brief borrows the *craft* of Accenture and McKinsey-grade decks, not their content or branding.

Deck rule: **no AI-market statistics.** Every number shown is ALPHV's own, verified, and carries a source line.

---

## 1. Narrative architecture

**Answer first.** Top-tier decks follow Minto's Pyramid Principle:
- State the governing thought first.
- Group three to five supporting arguments that don't overlap and cover everything (MECE).
- Then give the evidence.

The opening uses SCR / SCQA: a **Situation** the reader accepts, a **Complication** that disturbs it, the implied **Question**, and the **Answer**.

**Credentials inside a point of view.** "Who we are → what we do → clients" reads as a brochure. Leading firms argue a view first, so the firm becomes the obvious way to act on it:

**Why now → What we believe → How we help → Proof → How to engage**

Two tests for every draft:
- **Horizontal logic:** the action titles, read alone in order, tell the whole story.
- **Vertical logic:** everything on a slide proves its own title, and nothing else.

## 2. Slide craft conventions

**Action titles.** A full-sentence takeaway with a verb, at most two lines (about 15 words), sentence case, in a fixed position on every slide. The title is the "so what" and the body is the "because". If the body doesn't prove the title, rewrite the title.

**Tracker.** A small section label sits top-left (e.g. `2 WHAT WE BELIEVE`), or a row of sections with the active one in the accent colour. Same spot on every content slide. Leave it off the cover and dividers.

**Chart header and exhibits.** Under the title, state what is measured and the units: *"Handling time per case, minutes, before vs after"*. Number exhibits ("Exhibit 4") only if the deck will be read as a document. If you do, number them all.

**Footer stack.**
- Numbered notes, then `Source: ALPHV project data, client-reported, 2025`, then confidentiality marker and wordmark.
- Page number bottom-right.
- No number without a source. "Source: ALPHV analysis" is acceptable when true.

**Executive summary.** Three to five bold lead sentences in SCR order, each with one or two supporting lines. The bold text alone should give the whole deck. No imagery.

**Dividers.** Number, section name and at most one framing line. They are visually quiet pauses, not posters.

**"So what" callout.** At most one per slide, as a tinted box or rail headed *Implication*. It adds the consequence; it never repeats the title.

**One message per slide.** If a slide needs two titles, it is two slides.

**White space and grid.**
- One grid, fixed margins, everything left-aligned.
- Two body type sizes.
- One accent colour used only for emphasis; greys do the rest.
- Leave roughly a third of each slide empty.

**Charts** (Zelazny, *Say It With Charts*: message → comparison → form).
- Bars for ranking, lines for time series, columns for distribution, scatter for correlation.
- Pies only for share of a whole, with two or three slices.
- Highlight the series that carries the message and grey the rest.
- Label directly instead of using legends.
- No 3D or shadows. Bars start at zero, sorted. Keep decimals consistent.

**Case studies.** One fixed template for all ten:
- **Header tags:** industry · service · country · duration · products used.
- **Title:** the outcome as a sentence.
- **Three columns:** **Situation** (the problem in the client's terms), **Approach** (three or four steps, naming what was built), **Impact** (one to three hero numbers, each with baseline, metric definition and period).
- Use anonymised descriptors ("a Tier-1 Malaysian insurer") unless naming is approved in writing.
- An optional quote with role attribution.

**Capability house.**
- The roof is the client outcome.
- The pillars are the six services: parallel, MECE, the same grammar, three or four offerings each.
- The foundation is the shared enablers: delivery method, governance, data platform, products.
- One level of detail, equal box sizes, and no icons doing the explaining.

**Operating-model diagrams.** Chevrons run left to right, swimlanes show client and ALPHV roles, decision gates sit between phases, and outputs are listed under each phase. Arrows show flow only.

**Logo walls.** The title states what the wall proves. Group logos by industry, in greyscale, at equal optical size, and only with permission. Technology partners go on a separate slide, never mixed with clients.

**"Why us" without disparagement.**
- Compare delivery *archetypes* ("large global integrators", "platform vendors", "in-house build"), never named firms.
- Make the rows the client's buying criteria: time to production, team continuity, deployment control, local regulatory fit, cost to run.
- Score with Harvey balls.
- Concede where others are stronger, such as global multi-country scale. It makes the rest credible.

**Engagement model.** Three entry points: **Diagnostic sprint → Pilot → Scale and run**. Each stage shows duration, objective, deliverables, team, the gate that ends it, and the commercial form (fixed-fee diagnostic; milestone pilot; managed service to run).

**Closing.** End on decisions: next steps with owners and dates, then named contacts. No "Thank you" or "Questions?" slide.

## 3. Boutique positioning that beats big firms

Don't imitate breadth. Win on what leveraged pyramid models struggle to offer, and prove each point on a slide, not with an adjective.

- **Same team, start to finish.** "The people in this meeting build your system." Name them, and show team continuity in each case. This answers bait-and-switch worries without naming anyone.
- **Speed.** Lead cases with time to first production use, where true. Make the first step small, fast and fixed-fee.
- **Engineering depth.** Show what big-firm decks rarely do: real architecture, product screenshots, a live demo.
- **Sovereign and on-premises AI.** Deploy on client infrastructure or in-country cloud, with models and data under client control. This is the natural answer to residency and regulator concerns (slides 7 and 19).
- **Local fluency.** Malaysian and Singaporean data-protection law, sector regulators, public-sector procurement, and BM/English delivery. Check the current version of any framework before naming it.
- **Products as accelerators, not lock-in.** Present the products as reusable starting points that cut time and risk, and state IP and licensing terms plainly.
- **Coexistence.** Show ALPHV working alongside hyperscalers, vendors and incumbent integrators. That reassures procurement.
- **Tone.** Specific and evidence-led. Never "small but agile"; frame size as *focus*.

## 4. Amateur tells to avoid

- Topic titles; titles of three lines or more; titles that move position.
- Unsourced or "up to 90%" numbers; metrics with no baseline; market statistics lifted from vendor reports.
- Stock imagery: handshakes, glowing brains, robots, AI-generated people.
- Icon soup, clip art, gradients, shadows, 3D or many-slice pie charts.
- Walls of text, or more than six bullets.
- Buzzwords: "cutting-edge", "revolutionise", "seamless", "one-stop shop".
- Vendor logos passed off as clients; logos of mixed sizes and colours.
- Claiming expertise in everything; headcounts that differ between slides.
- Misalignment, mixed fonts, template leftovers, mixed spelling (keep to British English).
- Transitions and animations; ending on "Thank you".
